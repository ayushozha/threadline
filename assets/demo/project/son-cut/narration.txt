When my mum calls twice, I always wonder what I missed. Her question is simple: where are my glasses? But I can’t see the kitchen from here. I’m Daniel. This is Evelyn’s story.

The hard part isn’t the question. It’s losing the little bit of context that came before it. Threadline brings that context back, without asking Mum to learn another app.

Let me show you. This is Threadline. I’m opening a kitchen example in the Library. The clip is generated, but the next response comes from a real request.

I press Ask Gemini again. Threadline looks at the frames, describes what it can see, and chooses a next step. We wait for that response, instead of replacing it with a script.

And she can ask in her own language. Here, I ask in Nepali: where are my glasses? Gemini answers in Nepali, beside the same image. The words change. The support stays personal.

Now I ask, where are the glasses? The answer stays beside the scene, so we can check it. That’s the difference between a confident guess and help we can actually inspect.

Then there’s what happened before the camera was there. Here, I search Memory for the blue mug. Evelyn’s own words appear: she likes her tea by the window. I can open the source and see when she said it. Context, without making her repeat herself.

I can’t be beside Mum every minute. But a forgotten detail shouldn’t have to become a whole lost afternoon. Threadline. A little context. A little closer.
